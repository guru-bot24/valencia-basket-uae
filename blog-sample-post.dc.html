Inside a Youth Academy session

Every Youth Academy session at All Sports Arena runs on the same 75-minute structure used at L'Alqueria del Basket in Valencia. Here's what your child actually does — and why each phase exists.

Activation: every player, every ball

The first ten minutes look chaotic and sound loud — that's deliberate. Every player has a ball from the moment they step on court. Dribbling series, footwork patterns, and coordination work replace the standing-in-line warm-up. By minute ten, heart rates are up and hands are warm.

The skill block: taught by level

The middle of the session is the term's technical focus — this term, finishing off two feet. Players split into level-based groups, so a first-year player and a third-year player each work at the edge of their ability. Coaches rotate between groups with the same terminology used in Valencia.

"We coach the player they will be at eighteen, not the result on Saturday."

Concept play and competition

The final half is games — but games with a purpose. Small-sided formats (2v2, 3v3) force the session's concept to show up under pressure. The last fifteen minutes are full scrimmage with coaching stops: play fast, space the floor, make the next read.

Players leave tired, parents leave having watched every minute from courtside — and the curriculum moves one session forward, thirty-six weeks a season.
